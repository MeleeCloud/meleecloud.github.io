## A little context:

Like UNDERTALE before it, DELTARUNE takes the formula of a turn-based rpg, combines it with a very retro 2D pixel art aesthetic, but tweaks the victory condition into killing with kindness, but without the killing…I guess that’s just winning then. Every enemy the player encounters is defeated either by familiar rpg violence, or by interacting with it in specific ways to make it friendly and to show it mercy. Not only are the game mechanics tailored towards a more passive and defensive form of turn based combat, but it is greatly encouraged for the player to use mercy to fully experience the story and worldbuilding.

Much of the characters either come directly from or are inspired by their counterparts in UNDERTALE, retaining their overall personalities. However, the different context of the world of DELTARUNE as well as the lack of explicit connection to the previous game’s story allow these characters to feel fresh to both those who are and are not familiar with the previous game. What’s also nice is that DELTARUNE doesn’t rely too much on recurring characters to build its own story, and chooses to flesh out original characters for the sake of its own identity.

Speaking of characters, perhaps the most important character to consider is the player character themself, Kris. Despite the player controlling the actions of the main character, and given UNDERTALE’s track record for rewarding or punishing the player’s various actions, one of the core themes of DELTARUNE is that the player’s decisions ultimately do not matter. This establishes lots of depth to Kris, who, as a silent protagonist, has their character mostly expressed through the dialogue of other npcs.

## Thoughts and Impressions:

(This game has multiple chapters, so there’s no way I can cover EVERYTHING on this one page…I’ll dedicate a page to each chapter when the time comes.)

### The soundtrack

First off, I love the soundtrack…replaying Chapter 1 after like 8 years since it released and hearing “Field of Hopes and Dreams” again felt both nostalgic and exciting at the same time. Sure, I already knew tons of Chapter 1 by heart and was able to just speed through it, but you can only go so fast, and definitely not fast enough to ignore the Dark World vibes.

The initial music does a great job at fulfilling the fantasy adventure aesthetic that Chapter 1’s Dark World provides, and then as soon as Chapter 2 drops you back at Castle Town, it gets FUN. I could legit just let the game run for hours playing “My Castle Town” on loop while studying or something…such a groovy and chill theme. But that song was only a teaser for the rest of the musical diversity that I encountered throughout the game. Once I entered Cyber World and heard the funky electronic tracks, I knew the entire adventure that this game would provide would also be aesthetically diverse as I proceeded through later chapters.

### The gameplay

As for the gameplay? Uh… This game is so fucking easy, I really don’t know how my friends spent hours on the secret bosses…then again, either I’m just lucky af or the time I spent playing both rpgs and Touhou games actually paid off. (SPOILER: Fuck Sword Route tho.) I expected myself to die a lot more, but after beating every secret boss as of Chapter 5 in just 1-3 tries, I can confidently say that I’m pretty goated at this game.

Oh also holy SHIT grazing is soooooo satisfying. Every little “tick” sound is like a drop of dopamine injected directly into my brain cells, especially when there’s long strings of them from grazing projectiles that have multiple hitboxes. That, plus the TP bonus is such a great incentive for me to be ballsy with my maneuvering instead of playing it super safe. I’m glad Touhou taught me not to wait and hug edges, instead it taught me to go gung-ho through the line of fire to get from Point A to Point B.

My only real gripe is that some of the overworld hazards are actually way harder to avoid than the enemy bullets… which I guess is by design in some cases. At the very least, healing (in the overworld) isn’t usually an issue thanks to save points being located conveniently right before any major battles so you aren’t forced to use items to top off HP beforehand if you need to.

As someone who has played many a JRPG, I’m pretty grateful for how easy it is to get around in each Dark World, especially ones like Chapter 4’s which require some backtracking to unlock certain important things, but have map layouts that are simple and only place those secrets like two or three rooms away. The warp doors also make it very nice, as well as the amount of shortcuts provided (such as Chapter 2’s Queen’s Mansion and Chapter 4 with the flames). I do think the worst backtrack has to be Chapter 5’s pink coins unfortunately, since a lot are in the feather sections (which are still easy by the way, even the bouncy sections).

### Funnies

I think one thing I’m most glad about with this game is that it never stops trying to surprise me. I’m not just talking about witty dialogue that calls back to different points of the game and sparks a revelation in me; I’m saying this game REALLY loves to throw in unexpected in-your-face moments to keep you on your toes, whether or not they are fun (they mostly are). Probably one of the funniest experiences for me was watching the Tenna commercial at the start of Chapter 3 and telling my sister “I really hope he’s a 3D model” and dying of laughter as soon as I saw just how high quality and 60 fps he actually was compared to the rest of the game’s artstyle. Every appearance he made felt so uncanny and yet I loved every bit of it, especially seeing him dance in Castle Town as a looping gif.

Flowery is another character who activated a lot of neurons in my brain and made the game infinitely funnier. Forget the legit anime scene, as soon as I heard (what I’m assuming is) Toby Fox’s bitcrushed voice, it felt like I developed a vocal stim repeating every stupid quote he said. What made it even better was Ralsei getting ragebaited so hard that he continued to suggest turning off the voice lines in the options, only for Flowery to tease an option to turn off Ralsei’s dialogue instead for a whole scene. 

Before playing Chapter 2 and beyond I had barely any clue of who Dess was besides the little bit of exposure to DELTARUNE memes whenever Chapter 4 dropped all those years ago. Unfortunately, the most prevalent meme at the time was the stupid fucking Asgore drunk driving his truck song, which, with me being uninterested in the game at the time, made me think the entire time I was playing that Asgore hitting Dess with a truck was what actually happened. …Somehow, I was incredibly disappointed when I found out that was not the case and that the meme was actually the fandom being dumbasses. Forget the speculation that Dess is the Roaring Knight or whatever, no matter what ends up happening with her, I am STILL going to be sad that she actually didn’t get hit by Asgore’s truck because it means people were just memeing about nothing instead of memeing over what was supposed to be a serious moment.

### Story/Speculation

Funnies aside, the whole dynamic between Flowery and Ralsei in Chapter 5 was incredibly interesting and part of the reason why it was probably my favorite chapter so far; seeing Ralsei finally start crashing out after he had been either super wholesome or super serious, especially considering how he is 1) similar to Asriel and 2) Asriel = Flowey in the other game, really added to the fun of speculation about Asriel in this game as well as what would happen with Ralsei in the future. (SPOILER/SPECULATION: Seeing Ralsei’s reaction to the end of the prophecy in Chapter 3 and the imagery of his sprite having blood on his face makes me think that he will turn on Kris and Susie in the end and try to kill them, which is what he and Susie want to prevent.)

Also, back to the Kris thing…I really like how much the game stresses the struggle between Kris and the player character and even incorporates it into the gameplay of the beginning of Chapter 4. Probably the biggest evidence of Kris’s longing for autonomy is the abrupt lack of explanation text for some interactables, like Asriel’s diary and locker, or the door in the church, or the pictures with Noelle… one of them that stood out to me was the junk in Dess’s room that had Asriel’s retainer or whatever, but any subsequent interaction omitted the details entirely. It feels like Kris is an unreliable narrator for the player in the sense that they are choosing to omit details that the player might want to know for context, because they don’t want to talk about or think about it. I see the memes where people crossover Frisk and Kris and the SOUL immediately runs to Frisk like a reliable parent, and I get that it’s because Kris tries to keep the SOUL captive, but it also makes sense because Frisk would pretty much explain every detail in UNDERTALE (at least in non-genocide route) (probably because they are new to the Underground anyway).

(SPOILER) Oh and the Weird Route… I didn’t actually finish Chapter 2 with it, I died to Spamton Neo once and quit to do the normal route again. I did read up on the rest of it though, and I’m really interested in the fact that this route even exists despite the theme of “Your choices don’t matter,” which even the website FAQ says there’s only gonna be one ending. I do like that Chapter 3’s secret directly references it though (but fuck Sword Route I hate it and don’t wanna play it again). Maybe when the rest of the game is done and I’ve finished my main run, I’ll do Weird Route as a whole.

I really appreciate this game’s story for being piled with details and lore that fill in the gaps, but also for having heavy plot holes that encourage speculation using the pieces of the lore collected. The interactables in this game caught a lot of my attention and each world was small enough to make me feel immersed without wanting to just rush through them.


## Closing thoughts:

Although I’m really hoping Chapter 6 and 7 come out soon enough for me not to have to replay the rest of the game again to remember the story, after seeing the length of Chapter 5 I sincerely wish that enough time is dedicated to them to make them incredible experiences to close out the whole story. There’s been tons of speculation that I’ve seen online or made myself while playing, but no matter what choices they make with the rest of the story, I’m sure I’ll enjoy every single bit of it and find enough time to memorize all of it.

Even though I said the gameplay was really easy for me (or I probably used up all my generational luck), I kinda hope they don’t make anything more bullshit than it needs to be.. …I’m really just talking about Sword Route because as cool as the Zelda 1 gameplay is, and even though it is technically optional, it’s probably the biggest thing that is making me hesitate about considering replaying the whole game again. Besides that, I think that the difficulty of the regular bullet hell gameplay is very well tuned; as difficult as it gets later on with enemies dealing more damage, the game does a great job at training even the less experienced players to dodge increasingly complicated patterns. It might not be as ridiculous as Touhou, but the turn-based JRPG elements allow it to be balanced enough so players can be strategic with their resources and knowledge.
